> **How to use this template** (delete this block when you are done)
> 1. List every party that processes **your customers' personal data**: hosting, email, support tool, monitoring, AI services, backup. Parties that only see your own company data (for example your accountant) do not belong here.
> 2. Check your invoices and your product's integrations. Forgotten tools are the most common mistake.
> 3. Check for each party where the data really is. "EU" is only true if the contract or the settings say so.
> 4. Publish the list in a fixed place (for example your website or trust page), so you can refer to it in questionnaires.

## Official sources

Check the rules for sub-processors and transfers in the official texts. Adequacy decisions and standard contractual clauses can change.

- GDPR (Regulation (EU) 2016/679), article 28 (processors) and chapter V, articles 44 to 49 (transfers): [EUR-Lex](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2016/679/oj)
- Adequacy decisions of the European Commission, including the EU-US Data Privacy Framework: [European Commission](https://commission.europa.eu/law/law-topic/data-protection/international-dimension-data-protection/adequacy-decisions_en)
- Standard contractual clauses (SCCs): [European Commission](https://commission.europa.eu/law/law-topic/data-protection/international-dimension-data-protection/standard-contractual-clauses-scc_en)

## What a reviewer looks for

- **Completeness.** A reviewer compares the list with what they see in your product (for example a chat widget or analytics tool). If one is missing, trust in the whole questionnaire drops.
- **Location and transfers.** Especially for US suppliers: where is the data, and who can access it?
- **Agreements.** Whether there is a data processing agreement or similar arrangement for each party.
- **A date.** A list without a date looks forgotten.
